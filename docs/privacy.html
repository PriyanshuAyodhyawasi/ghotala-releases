Privacy Policy

Ghotala for Android · Last updated October 27, 2026

In short

Ghotala has no servers and no accounts. Your tasks and lists live on your phone. If you choose to connect Google Drive, backups go to your own Google Drive and nowhere else. The developer never receives, sees or stores your data.

Data stored on your device

Ghotala stores the tasks, daily list items, reminder times, completion history and settings you create in the app's private storage on your phone. This data is used only to show your lists, schedule reminders and calculate your score inside the app.

If Android's built-in backup is enabled on your phone, Android may copy this app data to your Google account as part of your device backup. That backup is managed by Android and Google under your Google account settings.

Optional Google Drive backup

You can choose to connect your Google account to back up Ghotala's data. If you do:

  • What Ghotala accesses: only files Ghotala itself creates in your Google Drive (the drive.file permission), plus its own hidden application data folder (drive.appdata), where backups made by older versions were kept. Ghotala cannot see, read, change or delete any of your other Drive files, photos or documents.
  • What it stores there: a "Ghotala" folder you can open in Drive at any time, even without the app: your daily list and tasks as readable files, each note as a file with its photos, drawings and voice notes next to it, your Vault (still encrypted), and full backups in "Ghotala/Backups" for restoring.
  • How it's used: solely to save backups when you tap "Back up now" or when you turn on automatic backup, and to restore them when you choose "Restore from Drive".
  • Sharing: backups are transferred directly between your phone and your Google Drive. They are not sent to the developer or to any third party, and are not used for advertising, analytics or AI training.
  • Your control: tap "Disconnect" in Ghotala to stop using Drive. You can delete Ghotala's backups and revoke access at any time in Google Drive (Settings → Manage apps) or at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Ghotala's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Password Vault

Logins, bank details, PINs and secure notes you save in the Vault are encrypted on your phone with AES-256. The key is derived from your Vault PIN and protected by your phone's hardware key store; fingerprint unlock uses a key that Android only releases after a successful fingerprint check. Your PIN and recovery code are never stored or sent anywhere. If the Vault is included in a backup (JSON or your own Google Drive), it stays encrypted and can only be opened with your PIN on the same phone or with your recovery code. Screenshots are blocked while the Vault is open.

Locked notes use the same Vault key: a locked note's text and list are stored encrypted, open only with your Vault PIN or fingerprint, and are blocked from screenshots while open. Only its title stays visible.

What Ghotala does not do

  • No advertising, analytics or tracking.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data.
  • No access to your contacts or location.
  • Lock In music (optional): if you allow "My music", Ghotala reads the list of songs saved on your phone so you can play them in Lock In. If you turn on "Lock In music controls" (Android's notification access), Ghotala only reads which song another music app is playing, so it can show it and pause, play or skip it. It does not read your other notifications, and none of this leaves your phone.
  • Diary: your diary pages, moods and diary photos stay in Ghotala's private storage on your phone (and in your own Google Drive backup if you connect it). You can lock the diary with your phone's fingerprint or screen lock. Nothing you write is read by anyone else, sent anywhere or analysed outside your phone.
  • Screen time (optional): if you turn on Android's "Usage access" for Ghotala, it reads how long each app was open each day, only to show your screen time and Day Score on the Score page. These numbers stay on your phone (and in your own Drive backup); Ghotala never sees what you did inside those apps.
  • Alarm: alarms, missions and your wake-up history stay on your phone. The camera is used only for the QR / barcode and photo missions; the code and the photos are checked on your phone by Google's on-device ML Kit (through Google Play services), and your reference photo stays in Ghotala's private storage. Physical activity and motion sensors count steps, shakes and squats. "Display over other apps" only brings the alarm screen back if it's closed while ringing.
  • Calendar (optional): if you turn on "Phone calendars", Ghotala reads (never changes) the events in calendars already on your phone to show them on its Calendar. They aren't copied or sent anywhere. If you pick your own songs for an alarm, Ghotala only gets permission to play the files you chose; they're never copied or uploaded. The talking alarm uses your phone's own text-to-speech. The flashlight is only switched on while an alarm rings.
  • Weather (optional): if you add your city, its map position (not you, not your location) is sent to Open-Meteo to get the morning weather.
  • Score insights: the observations on the Score page are worked out on your phone. No AI service is used.
  • Ask AI (export): if you tap "Ask AI about your month", Ghotala makes a file from the data you tick (diary writing and note titles are off unless you pick them) and opens Android's share menu. Nothing is sent until you pick an app; what that app does with it is covered by that app's own privacy policy.
  • Share to Ghotala: text you share from another app is only used to create the reminder or note you choose, on your phone.
  • Photos, the camera and the microphone are used only when you add a photo, drawing or voice clip to a note, and those files stay in Ghotala's private storage on your phone.

Permissions on your phone

Ghotala asks for notification, exact-alarm and battery-optimization permissions only so that reminders fire on time. It uses the internet only to:

  • back up to and restore from your Google Drive, if you connect it;
  • stream Lock In music from Ghotala's public GitHub page (tracks are cached on your phone);
  • check GitHub for a newer version of Ghotala, at most every few hours, and download and install it when you tap “Update now” (on Android 12 and newer, an update to Ghotala can install without an extra screen);
  • get the morning weather for the city you chose, from Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com), only if you added one;
  • download Google's public "Holidays in India" calendar for the Calendar, at most once a week (no account, nothing about you is sent);
  • translate text you choose (Hindi ↔ English) with MyMemory (mymemory.translated.net): only that text is sent, only when you tap Translate or turn on "Write it in English";
  • turn speech into text with your phone's own speech recognizer when you tap 🎙 (this works like your keyboard's voice typing; Ghotala keeps only the text);
  • download Lock In scenes and reminder or alarm sounds from Ghotala's public GitHub page, only when you choose them in Settings → Downloads, and read the list of what's available there.

These requests to GitHub send no personal data. Like any website, GitHub sees your IP address.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Ghotala removes all data on your phone. Drive backups can be deleted from Google Drive (Settings → Manage apps → Ghotala → Delete hidden app data).
